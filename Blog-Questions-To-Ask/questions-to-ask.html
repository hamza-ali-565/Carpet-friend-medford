15 Questions to Ask a Carpet Cleaning Company Before You Book in Medford

By Carpet Friend Medford | July 2026 | Jackson County, OR 10 min read

Before booking any carpet cleaner in Medford, ask: Are you IICRC certified? Do you pre-vacuum before extracting? Is the price confirmed before you start? What's the expected drying time in summer? Truck-mounted or portable equipment? These five screen out most low-quality operators. Given Medford's wildfire smoke, volcanic trail dust, and pet-heavy households, four more Medford-specific questions below are just as essential.

Why Asking the Right Questions Saves You Money and Frustration

We hear a version of the same story often enough that it has a pattern. A homeowner books whoever quoted the lowest price over the phone. The crew shows up, does something to the carpet, and it looks fine walking out the door that afternoon — damp but clean, no obvious problems. Then a week or two later, the traffic lanes start to look dingy again faster than they should. Or a musty smell shows up near a baseboard. Or the invoice at the door is fifty dollars higher than the phone quote, because "the carpet was more soiled than expected." None of this is unusual, and none of it required bad luck — it's the predictable result of booking on price alone without asking a handful of questions that would have surfaced the issue before a truck ever left the shop.

This isn't about turning a simple phone call into an interrogation. It's about five or six specific questions that take three or four minutes and separate operators who will do the job correctly from operators who are counting on you not knowing what to ask. For a straightforward job — a few rooms, no pets, no prior damage — the five foundation questions below are usually enough. For more complex situations — multi-pet households, wool or antique rugs, a home recently affected by wildfire smoke, a move-out clean that needs to satisfy a landlord — the additional questions further down are worth the extra few minutes, because the cost of getting one of those jobs wrong is a lot higher than the cost of asking.

The 5 Foundation Questions — Everyone Should Ask These

These five apply to essentially every carpet cleaning job in Medford, regardless of size or complexity. If a company gives strong answers to all five, that alone puts them ahead of most of the market.

1Are you IICRC certified?

Why it matters: The Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC) sets the industry's baseline training standard for carpet cleaning technicians — chemistry, fiber types, extraction methods, and safe handling of specialty situations. It's not a legal requirement to operate in Oregon, which is exactly why it's worth asking about: certification is a voluntary signal that a company invested in training rather than just buying equipment.

✅ Green flag answerThey state their certification number without hesitation and mention it covers general carpet cleaning, and often a specialty area like pet odor or upholstery.
🚨 Red flag answerA vague "yeah, we're certified" with no number, no specifics, and visible irritation at being asked to clarify.

2Do you pre-vacuum before extracting?

Why it matters: Dry soil — sand, dust, dander, volcanic ash from Rogue Valley trails, pollen — makes up a large share of what's actually in a carpet. Extraction alone, without dry-soil removal first, tends to turn that dry soil into mud that gets pushed deeper into the pad rather than lifted out.

✅ Green flag answer"Yes, always, with a commercial-grade upright before we apply any pre-spray." It's a standard, non-negotiable step in their process.
🚨 Red flag answer"We don't usually need to, the extraction gets it all." This skips a step that meaningfully affects the result, especially in homes with pets or high dry-soil load.

3Is the price confirmed before you start work — or can it change once you're there?

Why it matters: This is the single most common source of carpet cleaning complaints nationally: a low phone quote followed by a higher in-person price once the crew has already moved furniture and started setting up equipment, at which point most homeowners feel stuck paying it. Do not skip a cost discussion here without checking realistic ranges — see our Medford carpet cleaning cost guide before comparing quotes.

✅ Green flag answer"We confirm square footage and any add-ons before we start, and that's the number on your invoice — no surprises once we're working."
🚨 Red flag answerA suspiciously low flat number given over the phone with no mention of square footage, followed by "we'll take a look and finalize when we get there."

4What's your expected drying time in summer conditions?

Why it matters: Medford summers run hot and dry through the Bear Creek Valley, and carpet moisture management is different in July than in January. Extended dry time in summer heat isn't always about heat — it's often a sign of over-wetting, weak extraction suction, or poor airflow planning, and it raises the risk of wicking and mildew odor.

✅ Green flag answerA specific range — typically 4 to 8 hours with fans and good airflow — plus a suggestion to run ceiling fans or the AC afterward.
🚨 Red flag answer"It depends" with no range offered, or a shrug when asked directly — a sign drying time isn't something they actively manage.

5Do you use truck-mounted equipment or portable?

Why it matters: Truck-mounted systems draw power directly from the vehicle's engine, which generates significantly higher heat and suction than plug-in portable units. Higher heat and stronger extraction generally mean better soil removal and faster drying — portable units aren't automatically bad, but they're a different tool with different limits, and you should know which one is showing up.

✅ Green flag answerA direct, specific answer — "truck-mounted, we run at roughly 195°F water temperature for stronger extraction and faster dry times."
🚨 Red flag answerCan't answer clearly, or says "steam cleaning" and can't explain what that actually means when asked a follow-up question.

5 Medford-Specific Questions Most Homeowners Never Think to Ask

These five go beyond the generic checklist you'll find on most carpet cleaning sites. They're specific to conditions in Jackson County — wildfire season, volcanic soil types near Table Rocks and Roxy Ann Peak, and the documentation needs of a Rogue Valley rental market.

6Do you have a specific wildfire smoke protocol, or do you use standard extraction?

Why it matters: Smoke residue behaves differently than everyday soil — it carries volatile compounds that adsorb onto and absorb into carpet fiber, and standard extraction alone often leaves an odor that returns as the carpet warms. A company unfamiliar with wildfire smoke as a distinct issue is a company that hasn't dealt with Jackson County's actual conditions.

✅ Green flag answerThey describe an oxidizing pre-spray step specifically for smoke odor, separate from their standard process, and ask when the smoke event happened.
🚨 Red flag answer"We just clean it the normal way, that should handle it" — treating smoke odor as identical to everyday dirt.

7Are you familiar with the soil types in this area — volcanic clay, wildfire ash, Rogue Valley pollen?

Why it matters: Homes near Table Rocks and the foothills around Jacksonville and Gold Hill often see volcanic clay-heavy dust tracked in, which behaves differently under extraction than sandy or urban soil. A company that's worked across Jackson County will recognize this on sight rather than being surprised by it.

✅ Green flag answerThey mention adjusting pre-spray dwell time or agitation for heavier clay-based soil without being prompted.
🚨 Red flag answerNo recognition of the question at all, or a generic "dirt is dirt" response.

8Do you provide a dated service receipt suitable for Jackson County landlord-tenant documentation?

Why it matters: Move-out carpet cleaning is frequently tied to deposit disputes. A dated, itemized receipt showing professional cleaning was performed is often the deciding piece of documentation in those disputes.

✅ Green flag answer"Yes, every job gets a dated invoice with the address and services listed — we can email it same day if you need it for a landlord."
🚨 Red flag answerNo receipt offered by default, or a handwritten note with no company information on it.

9When do you recommend booking relative to Jackson County wildfire season?

Why it matters: Booking windows tighten up fast once fire season starts and smoke events hit the valley. A company that plans ahead with you — rather than only reacting after smoke has already settled in — is thinking about your home's actual annual conditions.

✅ Green flag answerThey suggest a pre-season clean in late spring and flag that post-event scheduling often needs a short wait for the AQI to clear first.
🚨 Red flag answerNo awareness that wildfire season affects scheduling or timing at all.

10Are you familiar with wool and natural fiber rugs — do you test for dye stability first?

Why it matters: Wool and other natural fibers can bleed dye or react poorly to the wrong chemistry. A dye-stability spot test before full treatment is standard practice for anyone experienced with these materials — skipping it is how rugs get permanently damaged.

✅ Green flag answer"We always spot-test an inconspicuous corner for colorfastness before treating a wool or natural fiber rug."
🚨 Red flag answer"We treat everything the same way" — no distinction made between synthetic carpet and natural fiber rugs.

5 Advanced Questions — For Complex or High-Value Jobs

These matter most when the job isn't routine: multiple pets, contamination that's soaked into the pad, an antique or high-value rug, or a situation where the outcome genuinely matters and you want to vet the company more carefully before committing.

11Do you use zone-specific enzyme formulas for multi-pet households — different chemistry for cat vs dog?

Why it matters: Cat and dog urine break down differently and often need different enzyme treatments to fully neutralize. A company that treats every pet stain identically is more likely to leave residual odor in a multi-pet home. See our detailed breakdown on removing dog and cat urine from carpet for how this works.

✅ Green flag answerThey ask which pets and how many, and describe using different enzyme formulations or dwell times accordingly.
🚨 Red flag answer"One spray works on all pet stains" with no distinction offered.

12Do you have sub-surface extraction capability for pad contamination?

Why it matters: When urine or other liquid soaks through to the carpet pad, surface-level cleaning won't reach it, and the odor typically returns within days. Sub-surface extraction tools are built specifically to reach and treat the pad layer, not just the fiber above it.

✅ Green flag answerThey describe a specific sub-surface tool or injection method for pad-level contamination and explain when it's needed versus standard cleaning.
🚨 Red flag answer"If it's in the pad there's not much we can do" without offering any deeper-extraction option at all.

13Are you insured — and does that cover antique or high-value rugs?

Why it matters: General liability insurance is standard, but not every policy specifically covers high-value or antique rugs. If something goes wrong with an heirloom piece, this distinction determines whether you have any recourse.

✅ Green flag answerThey confirm liability coverage and can speak specifically to how antique or high-value items are handled and protected.
🚨 Red flag answerGets defensive or vague when asked directly about insurance coverage.

14Can you provide references for similar jobs in Jackson County?

Why it matters: For a complex or high-stakes job, a reference for comparable work — similar rug type, similar contamination level, similar property size — tells you more than a generic star rating.

✅ Green flag answerThey can point to specific past jobs or reviews describing similar work, without hesitation.
🚨 Red flag answerNo references offered, or pushback on the request itself.

15What happens if I'm not satisfied with the result?

Why it matters: This question reveals how a company handles the rare job that doesn't go as planned. A confident, specific answer signals a company that stands behind its work; defensiveness signals the opposite.

✅ Green flag answerA clear satisfaction policy — they'll come back and re-treat problem areas at no extra charge within a defined window.
🚨 Red flag answerGets defensive, changes the subject, or implies dissatisfaction is always the customer's fault.

Red Flag Answers — When to Hang Up and Call Someone Else

Some answers are strong enough signals on their own that you don't need to keep evaluating the rest of the call. All examples below are generic and hypothetical — they describe patterns to watch for, not any specific real business.

🚨 Can't explain what IICRC stands for

If a company claims certification but can't name the organization or explain what it means, the claim likely isn't accurate.

🚨 A suspiciously low flat price before knowing square footage

A number quoted before any details are gathered is a placeholder designed to get you to book — not a real price.

🚨 Won't confirm price before starting work

"We'll figure it out when we get there" is the single most common setup for a bill that's higher than expected.

🚨 Says "steam cleaning" and can't explain what they mean

Most professional carpet cleaning is hot water extraction, not literal steam. An operator who can't explain the actual process may not understand it either.

🚨 Unfamiliar with wildfire smoke as a Medford-relevant issue

Given how often wildfire smoke affects Jackson County homes, a company with no awareness of it hasn't dealt with local conditions much.

🚨 Gets defensive when asked about satisfaction guarantees

Confidence in the work shows up as a clear, specific policy — defensiveness or dodging the question is a signal worth taking seriously.
