Hardwood vs Carpet Cleaning

Why Hardwood Floor Cleaning Requires a Different Approach

Can you use the same equipment on hardwood as you use on carpet?

Water and hardwood floors have a fundamental conflict. Excess moisture causes wood to swell, warp, and separate at the seams — a risk that increases with the amount of moisture and the time it spends in contact with the wood surface. This is why standard carpet cleaning equipment, which injects water at 195°F, is never used on hardwood floors. The steam and moisture would cause immediate and potentially permanent damage to the finish and the wood itself.

Our hardwood floor cleaning uses a flat-head applicator with pH-neutral wood floor cleaning solution, applied with controlled moisture — enough to emulsify surface contamination without penetrating the finish seal. The solution is immediately buffed dry, leaving no standing moisture on the surface. This is the IICRC-approved approach for finished hardwood cleaning.

Jackson County homes have a wide range of hardwood floor types — original fir and pine floors in Jacksonville's 1800s-era historic homes, engineered hardwood in East Medford's newer subdivisions, and reclaimed wood flooring becoming common in Ashland's renovations. Each requires awareness of the finish condition before any cleaning chemistry is applied. We assess finish integrity before starting.

Our Process

Each section is buffed dry immediately — no standing moisture. We inspect as we go for areas needing a second pass, then walk through the results with you.

Honest Assessment

What We Can and Can't Do for Your Hardwood Floors

Are there situations where hardwood cleaning isn't the right service?

We believe in telling you honestly what a service can and can't achieve. Here's our honest assessment.

What professional hardwood cleaning accomplishes

  • ✅ Removes accumulated surface grime, oils, and residue dulling the finish
  • ✅ Restores the floor's natural finish sheen where dulling is from surface contamination
  • ✅ Removes pet oils, cooking grease, and mineral deposits from mopping
  • ✅ Addresses wildfire smoke surface residue
  • ✅ Prepares the floor for re-coating if you're considering refinishing

What it does NOT accomplish

  • ❌ Repair scratches, gouges, or physical damage to the wood or finish
  • ❌ Restore finish that has been worn through to bare wood
  • ❌ Address water damage, cupping, or warping from prior moisture events
  • ❌ Replace the need for refinishing on floors with severely worn finish

If your hardwood needs refinishing rather than cleaning, we'll tell you during our pre-inspection. We'd rather refer you to a hardwood refinishing professional than clean a floor that won't look better after the service.

Do Jackson County conditions affect hardwood floors differently than other markets?
🏜️

Table Rocks Volcanic Dust

The fine silicate particulate from Table Rocks, Roxy Ann Peak, and Bear Creek Greenway trail access is particularly abrasive to hardwood finishes — harder than most household dust, acting as micro-grit sandpaper on polyurethane finish when walked across a floor. Homes with active trail users benefit most from professional cleaning, not just mopping, which distributes abrasive particles without removing them.

🔥

Wildfire Smoke Season Residue

Smoke-season PM2.5 residue (July–September) settles on hardwood surfaces and creates a dulling, hazy layer. This is different from normal grime and responds well to professional cleaning. If your floor looks consistently duller in fall than spring, wildfire season residue may be a contributing factor.

💧

Jackson County Hard Water Mopping

Medford's water supply has moderate hardness. Repeated mopping without rinsing introduces mineral deposits that build up over years into a characteristic haze. Many homeowners think their finish is worn when it's actually clean floor obscured by mineral haze — which cleans off in a single professional service.
